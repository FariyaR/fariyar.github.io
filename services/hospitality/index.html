Hospitality, restaurants and events · Enquiries and event quotes

Still answering booking enquiries at midnight and pricing events from last year’s spreadsheet?

  1. Enquiries answered with availability

    Inbox, WhatsApp Business API and Instagram DMs are read, the calendar checked in SevenRooms or ResDiary, or in OpenTable where its partner API is enabled, and a reply drafted with availability, the right menu and next steps, filed against the enquiry.

    Where a person decides: The manager approves every reply before it is sent.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Event quotes built from your price list

    An event enquiry becomes a drafted quote from your menus, price list, minimum spends and the last similar event, in your template, with the deposit invoice ready in Xero, QuickBooks or Stripe.

    Where a person decides: A quote goes out only after the manager edits it and taps send.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Deposit chasing that stops when it lands

    Follow-ups run until the deposit arrives and stop on reply or payment, with the booking confirmed in the calendar. Payment is read from Stripe or the bank feed in Xero or QuickBooks, so nobody checks by hand.

    Where a person decides: You set the wording and cadence.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  4. Function sheet built from the confirmed quote

    For venues running events from Word and email rather than Tripleseat, a confirmed quote produces the function sheet in your template: timings, menu, dietary needs, numbers, AV and floor plan, sent to the teams on the day you set and reissued on any change.

    Where a person decides: The events manager signs off each sheet before it goes to the teams.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  5. Enquiry pipeline with lost reasons

    Every enquiry is logged in Tripleseat, HubSpot or a sheet with source, value and status. When one is lost, a single short follow-up asks why and the answer is filed, so by quarter end you know what you lost and the reasons.

    Where a person decides: The sales lead reads the pipeline; the system changes no price.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  6. Corporate and event clients researched and approached

    Agents research local companies, agencies and event planners from their sites and recent events, score fit against your venue and capacity, and draft the approach in your words. A QA agent rejects weak drafts and sends them back, capped at two retries.

    Where a person decides: The events manager reads the passed draft and presses send. Rejected drafts show the reason.

    AI systemsComplex system · multi-week

Closest system I’ve shipped: LeadForge AI. Research, scoring and outreach agents behind a QA gate with capped retries is LeadForge, built for B2B sales, not a venue.

Hospitality, restaurants and events · Reservations, no-shows and covers

Still losing a Saturday eight-top to a no-show while the waitlist sits in a notebook?

  1. Cancelled tables offered to your regulars

    A cancellation in SevenRooms or ResDiary, or OpenTable with partner API access, messages the host’s own list for that slot on WhatsApp, regulars first; the first yes is booked and the rest are told. Platform alerts reach whoever opted in; this reaches guests you choose.

    Where a person decides: The host can pause it on a busy night or hold a table back.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  2. Pre-orders and dietary needs collected before arrival

    Parties above a size you set get a pre-order and dietary form a week out, chased once, and the kitchen gets one sheet per service with allergens flagged. Above a second size, a call task goes to the host instead of a reminder.

    Where a person decides: The head chef confirms the sheet; a person makes the call and owns allergen handling.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  3. Missed-call voicemails turned into booking drafts

    Where the phone system emails each voicemail with a transcript, as RingCentral and 3CX do, the email is read and a booking draft or callback task is created in SevenRooms or ResDiary with the caller’s number and request. No call audio is processed.

    Where a person decides: A person confirms every booking draft and makes the callback.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  4. Covers and sales forecast per service

    Bookings on the books, the same service in past weeks from the fact tables, and the local events you keep in a sheet give a covers and sales forecast per service each morning, posted with a prep list draft. The rota is written against it.

    Where a person decides: The chef and the GM set prep and hours; the forecast shows its inputs and last week’s error.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: OpenClaw Analytics Agent. Scheduled, logged messages from live platform APIs with non-expiring auth, running unattended, is OpenClaw, built for a marketing team, not a restaurant.

Hospitality, restaurants and events · Reviews, complaints and disputes

Still replying to last week’s reviews on Sunday night?

  1. Review responses drafted in your voice

    New reviews are pulled daily from Google’s API and the new-review emails TripAdvisor, OpenTable and Booking.com send; a response is drafted in your tone for each, anything serious flagged to the manager first. Google replies post after approval; the others are pasted by hand.

    Where a person decides: A person approves every response before it is posted.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  2. Review themes per site, weekly

    Every review, and the replies to the platform’s own post-visit survey from its export, is tagged by theme: service, food, noise, wait, cleanliness, value, with the quote kept. A weekly summary per site shows what moved and which dishes or staff were named.

    Where a person decides: The GM decides what to act on. Nothing about a named staff member goes beyond the manager.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Complaints routed to the duty manager

    The same classifier that sorts enquiries also spots complaints in DMs, email and the website form. A complaint reaches the duty manager within minutes with the booking and the bill from Lightspeed, Square or Toast, and the guest gets a holding reply.

    Where a person decides: The manager decides the remedy and writes the real reply.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  4. Card disputes answered with the evidence

    A chargeback on a no-show fee, deposit or bill in Stripe, Adyen or Square opens a case with the booking confirmation, your cancellation terms, the message trail and the itemised bill from the POS attached, and the response drafted in the processor’s format.

    Where a person decides: Finance decides whether to contest; nothing is submitted to the processor without a person.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: OpenClaw Analytics Agent. Daily pulls from live APIs with every item linked to its source and logged runs is OpenClaw, built for a marketing team, not hospitality.

Hospitality, restaurants and events · Supplier invoices and food cost

Still learning your food cost from the accountant, a month late?

  1. Invoices and delivery notes into the books

    Photos and PDFs of invoices and delivery notes extracted to line items, supplier and product matched through scan noise, and totals, VAT and duplicates checked before posting to Xero, Sage or QuickBooks.

    Where a person decides: The bookkeeper clears the flagged queue; nothing unmatched is posted.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Weekly food cost by site

    Purchase lines from the invoices and POS sales from Lightspeed, Square, Toast or Zonal’s export reconciled into a weekly cost-of-sales view per site, with stock counts from MarketMan or your count sheet, and supplier price changes carried in from the price tracker.

    Where a person decides: The GM decides on the menu; the report only shows.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Deliveries checked against what was ordered

    For kitchens ordering from a sheet or by email rather than MarketMan, each delivery note is compared line by line with the order. Short deliveries, substitutions and price differences are flagged the same day, with the credit note request drafted to the supplier.

    Where a person decides: The head chef decides what to query; the request goes out only when sent by a person.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. Unit prices tracked across suppliers

    Price per unit for every product and supplier is kept from the invoice lines, week by week. A rise above a threshold you set, or a cheaper supplier for the same item, is listed in one weekly message to the chef.

    Where a person decides: Buying decisions stay with the chef or the buyer.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  5. Dish margin from recipes and live prices

    Recipes from your spreadsheet or MarketMan are costed against current invoice prices and joined to the POS sales mix, so margin per dish and per menu is refreshed weekly instead of once a season.

    Where a person decides: The head chef and GM decide prices and the menu; the table only shows.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  6. Theoretical versus actual GP per site

    Recipes costed at current invoice prices, the POS sales mix, purchases and stock counts from MarketMan or your count sheets give theoretical and actual gross profit per site each period, with variance by product, so waste, over-portioning and missing stock show up by name.

    Where a person decides: The head chef and the ops director decide what to chase; the table only shows.

    Data systemsComplex system · multi-week

Closest system I’ve shipped: Tournament Document Intelligence. 412 records, 0 validation errors from mixed PDFs and scans, with a hard gate before anything is written. Tournament data, not invoices.

Still building next week’s rota on gut feel and fixing it on WhatsApp?

  1. Labour cost against covers, by day and site

    Rota hours and wage rates from Deputy, Planday, 7shifts or Fourth joined to forecast and actual covers from the booking platform and POS: labour percentage per site per day in one weekly view across all sites, against covers rather than sales alone.

    Where a person decides: The GM writes the rota. The view shows where last week’s hours went.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Open shifts filled on WhatsApp

    An open or dropped shift in Deputy, Planday or 7shifts messages eligible staff on WhatsApp in the order you set, reaching the ones who never open the app; the first yes is written back to the rota and the rest are told it is gone.

    Where a person decides: The manager approves swaps that break a rule, such as hours limits or skill cover.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  3. Clock-in hours reconciled to payroll

    Clock-in data from the rota tool is compared with the payroll export every pay run: missing punches, unpaid overtime, duplicate shifts and rate mismatches listed by person before payroll is submitted.

    Where a person decides: Payroll fixes each item; the system never changes a pay figure.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. New starter documents read and filed

    ID and right-to-work documents, bank details and signed contracts photographed by new staff are read by a vision model, checked for the fields payroll needs and for expiry dates, and filed to Fourth or your HR folder only after the check passes.

    Where a person decides: The manager clears anything that fails a check. No starter is cleared by the system.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: E-commerce Fulfillment ETL. Platform data into SQL with a column-by-column parity audit before cutover: 68,890 orders, 25 columns. A US retailer, not a restaurant.

Still opening four tabs before service to find out how yesterday went?

  1. One morning message before service

    An agent with tools over the POS, the booking platform and the reconciled tables posts covers, sales, spend per head, cancellations, review score and tonight’s bookings to Slack or WhatsApp at a set time each morning, every number linked to its source.

    Where a person decides: The GM reads and decides; finance reviews reconciliation differences weekly. The agent changes nothing.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. POS, bookings and payouts into SQL Server, nightly

    Lightspeed, Square or Toast by API, Zonal and Dojo from their exports, the booking platform and Stripe or Adyen payouts into staging tables; stored procedures build silver and fact tables per site, no-shows and cancellations included, reconciled to payouts and parity-audited against your accountant’s figures.

    Where a person decides: Finance or your accountant signs off the parity audit before the old report is switched off.

    Data systemsComplex system · multi-week
  3. Delivery platform statements reconciled to the POS

    The CSV statements Deliveroo, Uber Eats, Just Eat or DoorDash export are matched to POS orders line by line: commissions, refunds, missing-item claims and promotions, so the delivery margin per site is real, not the headline sales figure.

    Where a person decides: Finance reviews listed mismatches and decides what to dispute with the platform.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. Power BI per site on the fact tables

    Covers, sales mix, spend per head, no-show rate, labour percentage, food cost and delivery margin per site and per week in Power BI, reading the fact tables directly with scheduled refresh. It needs the nightly SQL Server build and the labour and food-cost tables first.

    Where a person decides: Owners and GMs agree the metric definitions once; the dashboard is read-only.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  5. “How did Saturday compare?” answered in Slack

    Owners ask in Slack or WhatsApp, in plain words, how Saturday compared with last year or which site’s spend per head dropped. The agent queries the fact tables and replies with the figures and the query it ran.

    Where a person decides: Read-only access. The query is shown so a person can check it.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: OpenClaw Analytics Agent. 4 live integrations, one morning message, every number linked to its source, running 24/7. Built for an EU marketing team, not a restaurant.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  2. Guest requests logged to housekeeping and maintenance

    A guest’s WhatsApp or email, extra towels or a broken shower, is classified, matched to the stay in the PMS and logged as a task in Mews, your housekeeping tool or a shared sheet where there is no API. The guest is told when done.

    Where a person decides: Anything urgent or a complaint goes to the duty manager at once.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. OTA bookings reconciled to the PMS and bank

    Booking.com, Expedia and Airbnb statements, from the extranet exports, matched to PMS reservations and bank payouts line by line: commission, no-show charges, cancellations and virtual card payments, with mismatches listed before the commission invoice is paid.

    Where a person decides: Finance decides what to dispute; the system matches and lists.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks

  1. Guest database built from bookings and POS

    SevenRooms by API or OpenTable from its guest export, the PMS, the POS and your email tool into one guest table: visits, spend, last visit, preferences and consent flags, the same person matched across systems through name and contact variations, synced to Mailchimp or Klaviyo.

    Where a person decides: Consent rules are set with you first. Nobody is messaged without a recorded opt-in.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Lapsed regulars invited back

    For OpenTable, ResDiary and PMS buyers, who lack SevenRooms’ built-in lapsed-guest emails: regulars not seen for a period you set get one message in your words by email or WhatsApp. A booking or reply stops it; the outcome is logged in the guest table.

    Where a person decides: The GM approves the wording and the list before each send.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  3. Captions drafted and the chef’s photos checked

    Photos the team drop in a shared folder are checked by a vision model against your written rules, crop, lighting, logo and no text over food, and a caption drafted in your tone for each. A batch is ready each week; no image is generated.

    Where a person decides: A photo that fails is parked with the reason. The owner approves every batch before anything is posted.

    AI systemsQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  4. Menus and allergen matrix from one recipe sheet

    The recipe spreadsheet or MarketMan export becomes the printed menu, the web menu and the allergen matrix in your templates, regenerated when a dish changes, so the three never disagree.

    Where a person decides: The head chef signs off every allergen matrix before it is printed or published.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
