Finance teams and lenders · Reconciliation and close

Still closing the month from three exports and a spreadsheet?

  1. Nightly reconciliation, audited column by column

    Ledger, billing and bank data (NetSuite, Xero, QuickBooks, Stripe, bank exports) into SQL Server staging, stored procedures building silver and fact tables, Power BI on top, and a column-by-column parity audit against your current reports before cutover.

    Where a person decides: Your team signs off the parity audit before anything replaces the old report; unmatched lines are queued with the reason.

    Data systemsComplex system · multi-week
  2. The weekly pack assembled before the meeting

    The numbers currently collected from four logins (ERP, bank, billing, CRM) become a scheduled report in Slack, Teams or email that lands before the meeting it was for, every figure linked to its source row.

    Where a person decides: The controller reads and signs off; nothing is posted by the job.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Stripe and Adyen payouts matched to invoices

    Processor payouts, fees, refunds and chargebacks from the Stripe, Adyen or GoCardless API matched line by line to invoices in NetSuite, Xero or QuickBooks, with unmatched amounts listed daily and the fee and FX lines drafted as a proposed journal.

    Where a person decides: Finance resolves the listed mismatches and posts the journal; the job never writes off or posts anything.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. Intercompany balances agreed before close

    Intercompany invoices and settlements from each entity’s ledger (NetSuite subsidiaries, separate Xero or QuickBooks files) loaded to SQL and matched both ways, with differences by pair and currency listed before the close starts.

    Where a person decides: Entity controllers agree each difference; the system lists, it does not post eliminations.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  5. Revenue recognition schedules rebuilt and audited

    Contract terms read from the signed PDFs, billing from Stripe, Chargebee or the ERP, and the deferred revenue schedule per contract rebuilt in SQL Server each night, audited line by line against the ledger’s deferred revenue balance before it replaces the spreadsheet.

    Where a person decides: Your controller sets the recognition rules and signs off the audit; the system computes, it does not post the journal.

    Data systemsComplex system · multi-week

Closest system I’ve shipped: E-commerce Fulfillment ETL. Built for a retailer’s finance team: 68,890 orders reconciled across 25 audited columns before cutover. Fulfilment data, not a ledger.

Finance teams and lenders · Invoices, statements and applications

Still keying supplier invoices and applications in from PDFs and scans?

  1. Invoices read into the ledger after checks

    Text PDFs and scans extracted, supplier and customer names matched through OCR noise, and totals, dates, duplicates and required fields checked before anything reaches the ledger in Xero, QuickBooks, NetSuite or Business Central.

    Where a person decides: Documents that fail a check wait for a person with the reason. Nothing posts without passing the gate.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Applications and statements into a case file

    Bank statements and application documents, text PDFs or scans, extracted into a fixed schema, running balances recomputed to catch a misread or missing line, and the figures cross-checked, so an analyst starts from a filled file, not a stack of PDFs.

    Where a person decides: Statements whose balances do not reconcile wait for a person; the credit decision stays with the analyst.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Remittance advices matched to open invoices

    Remittance advices forwarded as email or PDF, each in the customer’s own layout, read and matched to open receivables, partial and bundled payments included, with the cash application drafted in NetSuite, Xero or QuickBooks.

    Where a person decides: Cash is applied only after a person approves the proposed match.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. Document intake as an app you host

    Extraction, the escalating name resolver, the validation gate and an exceptions queue built as a Next.js and FastAPI application on hardened servers you control, EU-resident or your own cloud, with open-weight models self-hosted on GPU where documents may not reach a third-party API.

    Where a person decides: You set the residency rules before the build; your IT reviews the architecture; your team clears the exceptions queue.

    AI productsComplex system · multi-week

Closest system I’ve shipped: Tournament Document Intelligence. 412 records, 0 validation errors on mixed PDFs and scans, but on tournament data rather than financial documents.

Finance teams and lenders · Collections and receivables

Still chasing overdue invoices from a spreadsheet, when someone remembers?

  1. Reminder sequences drafted, approved, then sent

    Overdue invoices picked up from the ledger (Xero, QuickBooks, NetSuite), a reminder sequence drafted in your wording with the invoice and amount filled in, sent by email or the WhatsApp Business API, stopping on reply or on payment.

    Where a person decides: A person approves every reminder before it goes out. Replies land with a human.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Ageing digest without logging in

    A scheduled ageing summary to Slack or email: what is overdue, by how long, what was promised, so nobody builds it by hand.

    Where a person decides: The credit lead decides escalation; the digest informs.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  3. Replies sorted: dispute, promise or excuse

    Customer replies classified as dispute, query, promise, remittance or bounce. Promises are logged with date and amount in the ERP or CRM and chased the day after they are missed; disputes get the invoice and delivery evidence gathered and a reply drafted.

    Where a person decides: A person approves every outbound reply; the credit controller decides escalation; credit notes are never raised by the system.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. Customer risk scored from payment behaviour

    Days-to-pay, broken promises, disputes and exposure per customer from the ledger in SQL, scored against rules you write, with a weekly list of accounts to watch.

    Where a person decides: Credit limits and holds are set by the credit lead; the score advises.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  5. Collections agent with a QA gate

    Replaces the reminder sequence and reply sorting above with one system: agents sharing one typed state read the ledger, history and replies, pick the next action and draft it; a QA agent rejects anything off-policy, capped at two retries.

    Where a person decides: Every message waits for the credit team’s approval; any legal step is a human decision.

    AI systemsComplex system · multi-week

Closest system I’ve shipped: OpenClaw Analytics Agent. Scheduled, source-linked digests are the analytics agent pattern, built for a marketing team rather than a credit team.

Finance teams and lenders · Accounts payable

Still chasing invoice approvals by email and matching POs by eye?

  1. Three-way match before anything is keyed

    Invoice lines extracted from the PDF, before anyone keys them, matched to the purchase order and goods receipt in NetSuite, Business Central or SAP Business One, with quantity and price tolerances you set and every exception listed with its cause.

    Where a person decides: Within tolerance goes to approval; outside it waits for the buyer or the AP lead.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Approval routing with write-back to the ERP

    Each invoice routed from your delegation matrix (amount bands, cost centre, deputies) to the right approver in Slack, Teams or email, approved or queried with one click, and the decision, approver and timestamp written back to the invoice in NetSuite, Xero or Business Central.

    Where a person decides: Approval is a person’s click; the system routes, reminds and records.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  3. Supplier statements reconciled to the ledger

    Supplier statement PDFs, forwarded or fetched from the inbox, read and compared with your AP ledger, with missing invoices, duplicate payments and unapplied credits listed per supplier before the payment run.

    Where a person decides: The AP lead decides what to query; the system lists differences.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. Duplicate and fraud checks before posting

    New invoices checked against history in SQL for duplicates (same supplier, amount, date, or the same PDF twice), changed bank details, unusual amounts and first-time suppliers, with hits flagged before approval.

    Where a person decides: A flagged invoice waits for the AP lead; bank-detail changes require a call-back by a person.

    Data systemsQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  5. Supplier master deduplicated before the payment run

    The supplier list exported from the ERP run through an escalating matcher (exact, normalised, fuzzy) to find the same supplier entered three ways, with a merge list proposed and bank details compared across each duplicate set.

    Where a person decides: A person approves each merge; the job changes nothing in the ERP.

    AI systemsQuick win · days to 2 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: Tournament Document Intelligence. Supplier invoices are the same shape as that run: PDFs and scans in, 412 records out with 0 validation errors. Tournament data, not AP.

Still rebuilding the affordability spreadsheet from bank statements for every application?

  1. Affordability filled from statement transactions

    Income, rent, loan repayments, gambling and returned payments identified in the statements parsed under document intake or taken from Open Banking categories (TrueLayer, Plaid), averaged monthly and filled into your affordability template, each figure linked to the transactions behind it.

    Where a person decides: The underwriter checks the categorisation and owns the decision; the system fills the template.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. KYC file completed around Onfido or Veriff

    The identity result from Onfido or Veriff, proof of address and company documents gathered into one file; names, dates and addresses cross-checked against the application and the public company register; expiry and mismatch rules applied before the file is marked complete. No homegrown ID check.

    Where a person decides: Identity decisions and any escalation stay with the compliance officer.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Credit memo drafted from the file

    A first draft of the credit memo in your template from the extracted financials, the bureau report (Experian, Equifax, Creditsafe, from the API or the PDF you already pull) and the policy checks, each figure linked to its source document.

    Where a person decides: The analyst edits and signs the memo; credit committee decides.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. Policy rules checked before the analyst starts

    Hard policy rules (minimum trading time, maximum exposure, excluded sectors, LTV) applied to each application on arrival, with a pass or fail per rule and the evidence behind it.

    Where a person decides: A fail is a flag; the analyst can override with a logged reason.

    Data systemsQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  5. Broker submissions read and triaged

    Applications arriving from brokers by email or through your own portal’s webhook are matched to the broker and product, checked for missing documents against your checklist, and acknowledged with a list of what is still needed.

    Where a person decides: Acknowledgements use your template; anything that reads as a decision goes to a person.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  6. Underwriting workbench on your own servers

    A Next.js and FastAPI application for analysts: applications, parsed statements, policy checks, memo draft and decision log in one place, deployed on hardened servers you control, EU-resident where required, with open-weight models self-hosted on GPU so applicant data never reaches a third-party API.

    Where a person decides: Every decision is made and recorded by a named person in the workbench.

    AI productsComplex system · multi-week

    Bank receipts, GoCardless or direct debit files and the loan book (Mambu, LoanPro or the spreadsheet, via API or export) loaded to SQL Server, matched per loan and instalment, with arrears and balances rebuilt nightly and audited column by column against your loan system.

    Where a person decides: Finance signs off the parity audit before the nightly figures replace the spreadsheet.

    Data systemsComplex system · multi-week
  2. Failed payments handled on a schedule

    A failed direct debit, from the GoCardless or Stripe webhook or the bank’s return file, triggers the customer message in your approved wording and the case note in the loan system, with the sequence stopping the moment the payment lands. Retries stay with GoCardless.

    Where a person decides: Wording and timing are approved by compliance; a reply always reaches a person.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Arrears letters drafted and queued for review

    Regulatory arrears notices and letters generated from the loan data on the statutory day, in your approved templates, with the batch queued for sign-off before sending by post provider or email.

    Where a person decides: A person approves every batch; forbearance and default decisions are human.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. Arrears and exposure dashboard in Power BI

    Arrears buckets, roll rates, exposure and concentration per product and cohort from the reconciled loan tables, refreshed on schedule in Power BI, with movements between buckets flagged the day they occur.

    Where a person decides: The portfolio manager decides the action; the dashboard flags.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  5. Covenant tests from borrowers’ quarterly accounts

    For commercial lending: management accounts and compliance certificates arriving as PDFs are read, the covenant ratios (leverage, interest cover, LTV) recomputed against each facility’s terms, and a headroom or breach note produced per borrower, each figure linked to the page.

    Where a person decides: The relationship manager decides what to do about a breach; the system computes and flags.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  6. Borrower questions answered from the account

    Balance, next payment and statement requests from borrowers by email or on the WhatsApp Business API get a reply drafted from the loan record, in your wording.

    Where a person decides: The group controller owns the mapping and FX rates and signs off the parity audit against the Excel pack.

    Data systemsComplex system · multi-week
  2. Variance commentary drafted, numbers linked

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Daily cash position across every bank

Finance teams and lenders · Audit and controls

Still gathering audit evidence by hand, with AI tools that leave no trail?

  1. Audit evidence packs assembled on request

    For each sampled transaction, the invoice PDF, PO, approval trail, bank line and journal are pulled from the ERP, the document store (SharePoint, Google Drive) and the inbox into one indexed pack, with every step and source logged.

    Where a person decides: The controller reviews each pack before it goes to the auditors.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Journal and expense anomalies flagged weekly

    Manual journals, round amounts, period-end postings, reversals after close and expense claims outside policy checked against rules in SQL each week, from the ERP’s journal export, with hits listed by poster and account.

    Where a person decides: The controller decides what to investigate; the list is advice.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Control checks run and logged every day

    Segregation-of-duties conflicts from your role matrix, master-data edits and posting-period changes pulled from the audit log of NetSuite, Business Central or Sage Intacct daily, checked against your control list, and exceptions written to a log an auditor can read.

    Where a person decides: Exceptions go to the control owner; the system records, it does not block.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. User access reviews from ERP role exports

    Each quarter the user and role export from the ERP is compared with the HR leavers and movers list, every account with more access than its role allows, or belonging to someone who has left, listed for the owner to sign.

    Where a person decides: The control owner signs the review and removes access; the system lists, it changes no permissions.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: OpenClaw Analytics Agent. The analytics agent runs on a hardened EU server with logged tool calls, every number linked to its source. Marketing data, not a ledger.
