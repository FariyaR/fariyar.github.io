Still reading every CV by hand and retyping it into the ATS?

  1. CVs in any format read into the ATS

    PDFs, Word files, scans and LinkedIn profile exports extracted, names, dates and employers normalised with matching built for scan noise, then a validation gate before Bullhorn, Vincere or JobAdder is written to.

    Where a person decides: Files that fail wait with the reason; nothing is dropped silently.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Shortlist scored against written criteria

    Each candidate scored against the job criteria written down once, with the evidence for each score shown next to it. Scores are written to the ATS as a note, not a decision.

    Where a person decides: A recruiter reviews the scored shortlist before anyone is contacted or sent to a client.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Duplicate candidates merged across sources

    Your ATS already catches exact duplicates. This resolver catches the rest: name variants, typos, a changed email and a five-year-old record, escalating from exact match to fuzzy strategies, so one profile holds the full history from Indeed, LinkedIn and referrals.

    Where a person decides: Low-confidence matches are confirmed by a person once, then remembered. Nothing is merged on a guess.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. Applications filed to the right job

    Bullhorn, Vincere and JobAdder already pull Indeed and LinkedIn applies. This covers the rest: careers page forms, Reed, CV-Library and forwarded emails parsed, attached to the right job order, and knockout questions checked, so the recruiter opens a sorted list, not an inbox.

    Where a person decides: Knockout rules are yours. No candidate is rejected automatically; failed knockouts are listed for a recruiter.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  5. CV reformatted into your client template

    The parsed CV is laid out in your branded template in the order your clients expect, contact details removed, employment gaps and overlapping dates listed, and the client copy saved against the candidate in the ATS.

    Where a person decides: The consultant checks the formatted CV before it goes to a client. The candidate's wording is not changed.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: Tournament Document Intelligence. Same problem shape: mixed PDFs and scans with inconsistent names, 412 records and 0 validation errors. Tournament data, not CVs.

Still paying for job ads while the right candidate sits in your own database?

  1. Own database searched before the ad runs

    A new job order in Bullhorn, Vincere or JobAdder triggers a search of your own candidates by skills, location, availability and history, not keywords alone. Candidates are ranked with the evidence shown, and the list lands with the recruiter before any ad is paid for.

    Where a person decides: The recruiter decides who to call. The ranking is advice.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Research agents for candidates with public work

    For engineering, design and research roles, where candidates publish work: agents read a GitHub profile, portfolio, papers or website, score fit against the job spec, find the opening and draft the first message. A QA agent rejects weak drafts; the writer retries, capped at two.

    Where a person decides: The recruiter reads the QA-passed draft and presses send. LinkedIn profiles are not scraped.

    AI systemsComplex system · multi-week
  3. Dormant candidates re-engaged on schedule

    Candidates with no contact in a period you set get a check-in in your words, by email or the WhatsApp Business API. A reply stops the sequence and updates availability and status in the ATS.

    Where a person decides: You choose the wording and the gap. Replies land with a recruiter.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  4. Job ads drafted and posted after approval

    The job spec becomes an ad in your tone, checked against the wording your compliance team has banned, then posted through Broadbean or Idibu, the multiposter your ATS already uses, and logged against the job order.

    Where a person decides: A recruiter approves the ad text and the boards before it goes live.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: LeadForge AI. Six agents, one typed shared state, QA gate capped at two attempts. Built for B2B lead research, not candidate sourcing.

    Call notes, a recording transcript or the client's email become a job spec in your template: must-haves, nice-to-haves, salary band, process and timeline. The job order is created in Bullhorn, Vincere or JobAdder with the spec attached.

    Where a person decides: The consultant checks the spec with the client before anyone is sourced against it.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  2. Hiring signals researched and outreach drafted

    Agents watch company careers pages, public filings, press and funding news, research the company and the hiring manager, score fit against your ideal client and draft the approach. A QA agent rejects weak drafts, capped at two retries. Indeed and LinkedIn are not scraped.

    Where a person decides: The business developer reads the passed draft and presses send. Nothing is sent by the system.

    AI systemsComplex system · multi-week
  3. Terms of business sent, chased and filed

    Your terms go out from the template through DocuSign or PandaDoc with the agreed fee filled in, are chased until signed, and the signed copy is filed against the client in the ATS.

    Where a person decides: A director approves any fee or clause that differs from standard.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  4. Introduction log for back-door hire disputes

    Every CV sent to a client is logged with time, recipient, job and the terms in force, joined from ATS submissions and your sent mail in Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, so a back-door hire claim is answered from a record, not memory.

    Where a person decides: A director decides whether to raise a claim. The log shows what was sent; it argues nothing.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  5. Lapsed clients listed with a reason to call

    Clients with no job order in a period you set are pulled weekly from the ATS with their last placement, last contact and the consultant who owned them, and a drafted opening line for the call.

    Where a person decides: The consultant makes the call. No client is messaged by the system.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks

Still filling tomorrow's shifts by phone from a spreadsheet of who is free?

  1. Shift requests matched to compliant workers

    A client's shift request by email, portal export or WhatsApp becomes a shift in the ATS, matched to workers who are available, compliant for that site and within distance, offered on WhatsApp in your order of preference, and confirmed to the client when accepted.

    Where a person decides: The consultant sets preferences and can hold any shift. A worker with lapsed compliance is never offered one.

    AutomationComplex system · multi-week
  2. Worker availability collected weekly on WhatsApp

    Each week workers get a short WhatsApp message asking which days and shifts they can work. Replies are parsed into an availability table the shift matching reads, and silence is chased once.

    Where a person decides: The consultant sees who has not answered and calls them. Nothing is booked from availability alone.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  3. Equal-pay clock per temp worker

    The AWR 12-week qualifying period, or the equal-pay period your market sets, counted per worker from placements and timesheets in the ATS export, breaks and restarts applied by your rules, with the consultant warned two weeks before it is reached.

    Where a person decides: Your rules on breaks and comparators are written down once. A person changes the pay rate; the clock only warns.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  4. Fill rate and response time per client

    Shifts requested, filled, filled late and unfilled per client, site and week, with the time from request to confirmation, in Power BI from the shift records above or your ATS export.

    Where a person decides: Directors decide which clients get priority; the report only shows the history.

    Data systemsQuick win · days to 2 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: Creative Generation Pipeline. A scheduled n8n pipeline, every item checked against written rules, retries capped, 100+ a day unattended. Ad creatives, not shifts.

    Where a person decides: Finance signs off the parity audit before the new report replaces the old one.

    Data systemsComplex system · multi-week
  2. Timesheets read from photos and PDFs

  3. Timesheets chased before the payroll cut-off

    Where a person decides: Payroll decides what to do with the gaps; the system only chases and lists.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  4. Invoices drafted from approved hours

    For agencies billing from spreadsheets rather than a pay-and-bill suite: approved hours times the rate card per placement become draft invoices in Xero, Sage or QuickBooks with the timesheet attached, grouped the way each client asked.

    Where a person decides: Finance approves the batch before any invoice is sent. Rate changes are confirmed by a person.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  5. Margin per placement in Power BI

    Pay, bill and margin per placement, client, consultant and week, served from the reconciled placement and hours tables the first service builds, refreshed on schedule, so the Monday figure is the database figure.
