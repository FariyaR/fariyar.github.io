Marketing and advertising agencies · Client reporting

Still building Monday client reports by hand from GA4, Meta and Google Ads?

  1. Morning digest, one message per client

    An agent with tools over GA4, Meta, Google Ads and any other source with an API reads the last 24 hours and posts one narrative per account to Slack, every number linked to its source. EU-resident hosting if needed, read-only per client, tool calls logged.

    Where a person decides: The account manager reads it before anything reaches the client. No budget moves without a person.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Weekly client report drafted, not built

    The weekly deck becomes a drafted narrative with the charts attached, built from the same connections as the digest and laid into your template, ready for the account lead to edit.

    Where a person decides: The account lead edits and sends. Nothing goes to a client automatically.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Broken syncs caught before the client notices

    Expired tokens and stalled connections are detected and flagged the same morning, with non-expiring auth flows where the platform allows it, so a report never goes out on stale numbers.

    Where a person decides: A person fixes the connection; the system only alerts.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  4. CPA and conversion drift flagged between reports

    CPA, conversion rate and cost per lead checked daily against each client's agreed range, kept in one sheet per client, on the same connections as the digest. A breach posts to the account's Slack channel with the number and its source.

    Where a person decides: The account manager sets the ranges in the sheet. No budget is touched.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  5. Closed deals joined to the ad data

    HubSpot, Pipedrive or Salesforce deals matched to campaigns and leads in SQL, by click ID, form ID or email, so a client's report shows revenue and pipeline by campaign, not just leads and CPA.

    Where a person decides: Matching rules are agreed with the client. Unmatched deals are listed, not guessed.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: OpenClaw Analytics Agent. 4 live integrations, one morning Slack message, every number linked to its source. Built for an in-house marketing team, not an agency.

Marketing and advertising agencies · Creative production

Still waiting on one designer to review every ad variation?

  1. Ad creative generated and QA-checked on schedule

    Brief, cached prompt, image generation, then a vision model checks each image against the client's written brand rules and regenerates on failure, capped. Runs on a schedule in n8n whether anyone is watching or not.

    Where a person decides: A creative that fails twice is parked. A person approves the batch before it enters the ad account.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Brand rules written down once, applied every time

    Logo placement, palette and text density become explicit rules a vision model enforces on every creative, designer-made or generated, dropped into a shared folder. Each carries its verdict and the rule it passed or failed, so the review is of exceptions, not the whole batch.

    Where a person decides: The creative director owns the rules and can change them any time.

    AI systemsQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  3. Per-client pipelines, paused-ad upload, cost per creative logged

    One n8n pipeline with a rulebook, cached prompt and output folder per client. Batches land in each client's Meta ad account as paused ads, or in the Google Ads asset library where its API allows, with the QA verdict and cost per creative logged.

    Where a person decides: Each client's rulebook is signed off by the creative director. A person sets ads live.

    AI systemsComplex system · multi-week
  4. One approved creative adapted to every placement

    An approved master is resized and re-laid-out for feed, story, reel and display sizes with outpainting where the crop needs it. A vision check confirms the logo and text survived before the set is delivered to the shared folder.

    Where a person decides: A designer reviews any size the check flags. The set ships only after that.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  5. Ad copy drafted and checked against claims rules

    Headlines, primary text and descriptions drafted from the brief as structured output with platform limits in the schema, 30-character headlines and 90-character descriptions, then checked against the client's claims list and banned words. Variants that fail are rewritten once, then parked.

    Where a person decides: The copywriter picks and edits from the passed variants. Nothing goes live from here.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  6. Creative attributes tagged, joined to performance

    A vision model tags every live ad pulled through the Meta and Google Ads APIs: format, hook, colour, people, text amount, offer. Tags are joined to spend and results in SQL so the next brief starts from what won, with the numbers.

    Where a person decides: The strategist writes the brief; the tags and numbers are input, not a decision.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: Creative Generation Pipeline. 100+ QA-checked creatives a day at 78% lower inference cost, Meta ads for an EU education brand, not an agency.

Still researching prospects one account at a time, by hand?

  1. Prospect research and outreach drafts, QA-gated

    Coordinated agents with one typed shared state research a prospect, read its website, score it against your ideal client profile, find the opening and draft the outreach. A QA agent rejects weak drafts with reasons and the writer retries, capped at two attempts.

    Where a person decides: You review the QA-passed draft and press send. Rejected drafts show the reason.

    AI systemsComplex system · multi-week
  2. Leads scored and routed into the CRM

    Inbound enquiries from your site form and inbox, and researched prospects, land in HubSpot or Pipedrive scored against your ideal client profile, with the research attached, so the first call starts informed.

    Where a person decides: Your team decides who to pursue; the score is advice.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. RFPs and briefs read into a go/no-go sheet

    RFP PDFs and inbound briefs extracted to scope, budget, deadlines, deliverables and mandatory requirements in one fixed schema, with gaps listed. Where you keep a library of past responses, the matching answer is pulled in beside each question.

    Where a person decides: The new business lead decides go or no-go from the sheet.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. Proposal drafted from the discovery call

    The call transcript from Zoom, Google Meet or Fireflies becomes a structured scope, then a proposal in your template with services and prices from your rate card, filed to the deal in the CRM. The same transcript intake feeds the meeting-notes service in onboarding.

    Where a person decides: You edit the scope and the price before anything is sent.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  5. Proposal follow-ups that stop on reply

    A sent proposal starts a follow-up sequence in your wording from your mailbox, with the deal stage updated in HubSpot or Pipedrive. A reply from the prospect stops the sequence and lands with you.

    Where a person decides: You choose the wording and schedule. Every reply reaches a person.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  6. Self-serve ad account audit as a lead magnet

    A prospect connects Meta or Google Ads on your site, a worker runs your audit checks as a queued job, and a branded report comes back with the lead in your CRM. Meta App Review and Google Ads API approval come first and take weeks.

    Where a person decides: You own the checks and the report wording. Sales follows up by hand.

    AI productsComplex system · multi-week

Closest system I’ve shipped: LeadForge AI. 6 agents, one typed state, QA gate capped at two attempts, retries visible in the UI. General lead research, not agency new business.

Marketing and advertising agencies · Campaign operations

Still checking UTMs, naming and pacing by hand across every client account?

  1. Launch QA before a campaign goes live

    Campaigns built paused in Meta or Google Ads are checked against the client's checklist: naming convention, UTMs, conversion events, geo, budget caps, landing page response. A pass or fail per rule is posted to the account's channel before anyone sets it live.

    Where a person decides: The media buyer fixes and sets live. The system activates nothing.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Budget pacing watched every hour

    Spend per client pulled hourly from the Meta and Google Ads APIs and compared with the monthly plan in one sheet per client. Projected overspend or underspend is posted to the account's channel with the figures.

    Where a person decides: No budget changes without a person. The alert only informs.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  3. Search terms reviewed with negatives proposed

    Google Ads search term reports read weekly through the API, each term classified against the client's offer, and negative keyword candidates listed with the reason and the spend behind each. Approved negatives are written back to the right list.

    Where a person decides: The buyer approves each negative before it is added to the account.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. Rule-driven change proposals with the numbers attached

    Triggers agreed per client, such as CPA over target for three days at a set spend, or frequency past a cap. When one fires, the proposal is written with the figures, the window and the query behind them, and posted for a decision.

    Where a person decides: The buyer accepts or rejects each proposal. Nothing edits an account from here.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  5. Approved changes applied through the ads APIs

    An approval queue in Slack. An accepted change is applied through the Meta or Google Ads API one write at a time, before and after state logged. A failed write stops the batch and the writes already made are returned to their logged before-state.

    Where a person decides: Every change is approved by a named person, with a cap per change per client.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  6. Server-side conversions sent and watched per client

    Order or lead events from the client's Shopify, WooCommerce or HubSpot webhook sent to the Meta Conversions API and Google Ads enhanced conversions through n8n. A daily count compares events accepted by each platform with the site's own total and flags the gap.

    Where a person decides: You decide which events each client sends. A flagged gap goes to the media buyer.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: OpenClaw Analytics Agent. The agent reads Meta, Google Ads and GA4 live and only reports to Slack. Built for an in-house team, not an agency.

Marketing and advertising agencies · Content and SEO

Still writing SEO briefs by hand and pasting drafts into WordPress one by one?

  1. Content briefs built from Search Console and SERPs

    For a target keyword the brief pulls the client's current ranking pages from the Search Console API, the competing page outlines and the questions people ask from a SERP API such as DataForSEO, into your brief template.

    Where a person decides: The strategist approves the brief before a writer or a model starts.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Drafts written and QA-checked against the brief

    A writer agent drafts from the approved brief and the client's tone rules. A QA agent checks structure, claims and banned phrases, rejects with reasons, and the writer retries, capped at two. Drafts land in Google Docs with the QA notes.

    Where a person decides: The editor reviews only drafts that passed QA. Failed drafts show the reason.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Approved articles loaded into the CMS as drafts

    Approved copy goes into WordPress, Webflow or HubSpot through its API as a draft with title, meta description, headings, internal links and images in place. One CMS per build. Nothing is published by the system.

    Where a person decides: A person presses publish in the CMS.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  4. Ranking and index drops flagged weekly

    Search Console data for each client site pulled weekly through the API and joined to a Screaming Frog crawl export. Pages that lost position, dropped out of the index or returned errors are listed with the change.

    Where a person decides: The SEO lead decides what to fix. The report only shows.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  5. Content audit: decay, cannibalisation, orphans

    Sixteen months of Search Console history joined to the Screaming Frog crawl in SQL. Pages losing clicks, two pages competing for one query, orphans and thin pages are scored and listed with the evidence, so the rewrite and merge list is data, not opinion.

    Where a person decides: You choose which pages to rewrite or merge; the list is advice.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: LeadForge AI. A writer agent and a QA agent that rejects with reasons and retries, capped at two. Built for outreach copy, not articles.

Marketing and advertising agencies · Social inboxes and community

Still answering client DMs and comments from six logins, whenever someone checks?

  1. DMs and comments triaged into one queue

    Instagram and Facebook messages and comments for every client pulled through Meta's APIs, classified as question, complaint, lead or spam, and routed to the right person. LinkedIn and TikTok only through an inbox tool with an API, such as Sprout Social; otherwise they stay manual.

    Where a person decides: Complaints go to a person straight away. Routing rules are yours.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Replies drafted from each client's FAQ and tone

    Each queued Instagram or Facebook item gets a draft from that client's FAQ, offers and tone rules, with the thread attached, in the queue where the community manager works.

    Where a person decides: The community manager approves every reply before it posts.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Social leads handed to the client's CRM

    A DM that asks about price, availability or a booking is logged as a lead in the client's HubSpot, Pipedrive or Google Sheet, with the conversation attached and the source channel recorded.

    Where a person decides: The client's team follows up. Nothing is sold by the machine.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  4. Comment moderation with a review queue

    Comments are checked against each client's written rules. Clear spam and abuse are hidden through the API, which Facebook and Instagram allow; borderline items wait in a queue with the rule that was nearly broken. Other platforms get the queue, not the hide.

    Where a person decides: Nothing is deleted. A person reviews the queue and the hidden list.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  5. Weekly community digest per client

    One message per client each week from the same Meta connections: themes in the comments, repeated questions, complaints and the mentions that still need a reply, each linked to the post.

    Where a person decides: The account manager reads it and decides what to raise.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: OpenClaw Analytics Agent. Unattended agent over live Meta and Google APIs, tool calls logged, EU-hosted. Built to read an in-house team's numbers, not answer inboxes.

Marketing and advertising agencies · Agency data and profitability

Still finding out which retainers made money from Xero and a timesheet export?

  1. Every client's ad data in one SQL Server

    Meta, Google Ads, GA4, TikTok and LinkedIn Ads pulled nightly through n8n into staging tables, stored procedures building silver and fact tables per client with metric definitions written down, a parity check against each platform's UI, and Power BI on top.

    Where a person decides: Metric definitions are agreed with you. The platform UI stays the reference until parity is signed off.

    Data systemsComplex system · multi-week
  2. Historical exports backfilled into the same tables

    Old CSV exports from each platform, in whatever layout they were saved, loaded into the same staging and fact tables. Schema drift, duplicates, currencies and renamed campaigns are handled by explicit rules, not filtered by hand. Usually the first phase of the warehouse build.

    Where a person decides: Each handling rule is agreed with you before it is applied.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Media spend reconciled against platform invoices

    Spend in the fact tables matched line by line to Meta and Google invoice PDFs and to what was rebilled in Xero or QuickBooks, with every difference listed by client, account and month.

    Where a person decides: Finance resolves each listed mismatch. Nothing is averaged away.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. Retainer margin per client, refreshed nightly

    Hours from Harvest, Toggl or Clockify, fees and pass-through costs from Xero or QuickBooks, joined per client and service line in SQL Server into a margin view in Power BI, with your allocation rules applied and shown.

    Where a person decides: You set the rates and allocation rules. The report is read-only.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  5. Monthly media invoices drafted in Xero

    On the first of the month, each client's invoice is drafted in Xero or QuickBooks from reconciled spend plus the agreed fee or percentage, with the platform invoice PDFs attached as backup and any unresolved mismatch held back.

    Where a person decides: Finance approves and sends every invoice. A held-back invoice shows the mismatch.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
