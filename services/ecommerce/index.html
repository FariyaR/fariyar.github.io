Still closing the month from Shopify and 3PL exports that never agree?

  1. Shopify and 3PL into SQL Server, nightly

    Shopify and ShipHero by API, or your 3PL’s export, into staging tables; stored procedures build silver and fact tables; Power BI reads the fact tables. Voided shipments and double-counted orders handled explicitly.

    Where a person decides: A column-by-column parity audit runs with your finance or QC team before cutover. They sign off.

    Data systemsComplex system · multi-week
  2. Payouts reconciled against orders

    Shopify Payments, PayPal, Stripe and Klarna payouts pulled by API and matched to orders, refunds and fees line by line, so the bank balance and the sales report agree. Mismatches listed with the order.

    Where a person decides: Finance reviews listed mismatches; nothing is averaged away.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. 3PL invoices checked against shipments

    Pick, pack, storage and postage charges from the ShipHero billing export or your 3PL’s invoice PDF, matched to shipped orders and stock in the fact tables. Charges with no shipment, double charges and rate differences are listed.

    Where a person decides: You decide what to dispute with the 3PL. The system only lists.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. Month-end pack assembled from the fact tables

    Revenue by channel and country, refunds, fees, COGS and ad spend built from the fact tables into the sheet and PDF your accountant expects, on the first of the month. Needs the nightly sync above in place first.

    Where a person decides: The accountant reads and posts. The job writes nothing to Xero or QuickBooks.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  5. Marketplace settlements in the same tables

    Amazon settlement reports through SP-API, and eBay or Etsy payout exports, loaded nightly beside Shopify with fees, reserves and returns broken out, so every channel reconciles the same way.

    Where a person decides: Finance signs off the first month side by side with the old report.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  6. Refunds matched to returns received

    Refunds and exchanges in Shopify, Loop or your returns app matched to items ShipHero logged as received and restocked. Refunds with no return, returns with no refund, and items received but not restocked are listed each week. Built on the fact tables.

    Where a person decides: Finance and the 3PL contact work the list. Nothing is written back to Shopify.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: E-commerce Fulfillment ETL. Built for a US retailer’s finance team: 68,890 orders reconciled across 25 audited columns, synced nightly. Same platforms, same problem.

Still opening four tabs every morning to find out how yesterday went?

  1. Yesterday’s numbers in Slack before the team starts

    An agent with tools over Shopify, GA4, Meta and Google Ads pulls the last 24 hours, reasons over them and posts one narrative every morning, every number linked to its source. A Monday weekly version in your template is an option.

    Where a person decides: The team reads it. The agent moves no budget and changes nothing in the ad accounts.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Contribution margin per day, in the digest

    Revenue less COGS, shipping, processor fees and ad spend from Meta and Google, computed per day and per channel in SQL and added to the morning message. COGS per SKU lives in Shopify’s cost field or a sheet you own, loaded nightly.

    Where a person decides: Finance signs off the cost definitions once. The figures are read-only.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Questions answered from the tables in Slack

    Ask in Slack how a SKU did in Germany last week; an agent with a read-only SQL tool over the fact tables answers with the number and the query it ran.

    Where a person decides: Read-only access. Anyone can check the query behind an answer.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. Tracking gaps and spend swings caught daily

    Each morning Shopify orders are compared with GA4 purchases and Meta’s reported conversions, and spend, CPA and refund rate with the last four weeks. When a gap or a number leaves its usual range, a message names it and the source.

    Where a person decides: You set the ranges. The check posts to Slack; budgets and pixels are not touched.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: OpenClaw Analytics Agent. 4 live integrations, one morning message, running 24/7 for an EU marketing team. GA4, Meta and Google Ads there; Shopify was not among them.

    The WISMO slice on its own: a Gorgias, Zendesk or Shopify Inbox ticket matched to the order, shipment status pulled from ShipHero and the carrier’s tracking API, and a reply drafted in your tone with the dates in it.

    Where a person decides: An agent approves replies. Auto-send only for the categories you allow.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Refunds released only after the 3PL receipt

    Loop or Shopify handles eligibility and labels. This adds what they cannot: the refund is held until ShipHero logs the item received and its condition, and the customer’s return history and open tickets are checked before it goes out.

    Where a person decides: Refunds above a limit you set, damaged returns and repeat returners wait for a person.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Chargeback evidence packs assembled from your systems

    For each dispute in Stripe, Shopify Payments or PayPal, the order, tracking, delivery proof, policy text and customer messages are collected into one pack in the processor’s format.

    Where a person decides: A person reviews the pack and submits it.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  4. Support agent with typed state and QA gate

    The full version of the WISMO slice above: a multi-step agent reads the ticket, looks up the order and shipment, checks your policy, drafts a reply and hands it to a QA agent that checks policy and tone, retries capped at two.

    Where a person decides: Refunds, complaints and anything the policy does not cover go to a named person.

    AI systemsComplex system · multi-week

Closest system I’ve shipped: LeadForge AI. Drafting behind a QA gate with capped retries and one typed state is the LeadForge pattern. Built for B2B outreach, not support.

  3. Flow copy drafted in your voice and checked

    Welcome, post-purchase, win-back and browse-abandon copy drafted from your brand rules and best reviews, in structured form, checked against your claims list before it reaches Klaviyo as a draft.
