Logistics, fulfilment and 3PL · WMS data and client reporting

Still exporting the WMS every Monday to find out why the order count doesn’t match finance?

  1. WMS to SQL Server, reconciled every night

    ShipHero or your WMS into staging tables through n8n and the API, stored procedures building silver and fact tables, Power BI on top. Voided and duplicated shipments handled explicitly, not filtered by hand.

    Where a person decides: Your QC or finance team signs off the parity audit before cutover. Failed rows are queued with the reason.

    Data systemsComplex system · multi-week
  2. Client reports built and sent on schedule

    Per-client volume, SLA, returns and billing reports built from the fact tables of the nightly sync above and sent on the day they are due, every number linked to its source row. Return rates and claim outcomes are columns in the same tables.

    Where a person decides: The account manager reviews before the first send to each client; after that it runs unless a check fails.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. SLA tracking from order to carrier scan

    Received, picked, packed and shipped timestamps from the WMS, first carrier scan from ShipStation or the carrier’s tracking API, in one table per client. Same-day and next-day hit rates computed daily, misses listed by order. Each carrier feed is scoped separately.

    Where a person decides: You set the SLA definitions once. The table shows misses; the account manager decides what to tell the client.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. Client-facing Power BI with row-level security

    One Power BI model on the fact tables, row-level security so each client sees only their own orders, stock and SLA numbers. Scheduled refresh, failures logged rather than hidden. The lighter option; the client portal under onboarding adds invoices and an assistant.

    Where a person decides: You decide which metrics clients may see; definitions are written down once and reviewed by you.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  5. Morning ops digest in Slack or Teams

    An agent reads yesterday’s orders, backlog, late shipments and stock-outs from the fact tables or the ShipHero API and posts one short narrative at a set time, every number linked to its source.

    Where a person decides: The ops lead reads it and decides. The agent moves no orders and changes nothing in the WMS.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  6. WMS migration audited column by column

    Moving from a legacy WMS to ShipHero or another platform: orders, stock, clients and SKUs pulled from both systems by API or export into SQL Server and compared column by column before cutover, differences listed by cause. The vendor migrates; this proves it landed.

    Where a person decides: Your ops and finance leads sign off the parity report before the old WMS is switched off.

    Data systemsComplex system · multi-week

Closest system I’ve shipped: E-commerce Fulfillment ETL. Same data, same stack: ShipHero fulfilment data into SQL Server, 68,890 orders reconciled across 25 audited columns, nightly. A retailer, not a 3PL.

Still retyping packing lists and bills of lading from scanned PDFs?

  1. Packing lists and PODs read into validated records

    Packing lists, BOLs and PODs from the shared inbox or an SFTP drop, as HTML, text PDFs or scans, read by text and vision models. SKUs and customers matched through OCR noise, then a hard validation gate before anything reaches the WMS. Can run EU-resident.

    Where a person decides: Records that fail the gate wait for a person with the reason attached.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Client order sheets imported, not keyed

    Spreadsheets in each client’s own layout mapped to your schema once, then imported into the WMS on every arrival with the same checks. SKUs resolve through the mapping confirmed at onboarding, not a second matcher.

    Where a person decides: New layouts are confirmed by a person before the mapping is saved.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Receiving discrepancies flagged at the dock

    The supplier’s packing list or ASN, already extracted above, compared line by line with the receiving count in ShipHero through its API. Short, over and damaged lines listed the same day in Slack or email with the document attached.

    Where a person decides: The receiving lead decides what to raise with the client or supplier; the system only flags.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. Dock bookings drafted from inbound emails

    Inbound notices and attached BOLs are read, the container or pallet count and expected date pulled out, and a booking drafted in Google Calendar or Outlook, or as the expected date on the purchase order in ShipHero.

    Where a person decides: The warehouse manager confirms every booking; nothing is scheduled without a person.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: Tournament Document Intelligence. 412 records, 0 validation errors on mixed HTML, PDF and scanned input, behind a hard validation gate. Tournament data, not freight paperwork.

Still quoting rates and chasing carrier exceptions by hand from a shared inbox?

  1. RFQs quoted from your rate card

    An RFQ arrives by email or PDF; the quote is drafted from the rate card table below and your lead times, in your template, and filed against the client in HubSpot or Pipedrive. A QA check rejects incomplete drafts before you see them.

    Where a person decides: A person approves every quote before it is sent.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Carrier exceptions triaged and answered

    Exception emails from UPS, FedEx, DHL or DPD are classified, matched to the order, and a reply or escalation drafted with the order status pulled from ShipHero or ShipStation.

    Where a person decides: Replies to clients are approved by a person; the system drafts and files.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Where-is-my-order replies drafted from tracking

    Client and end-customer WISMO emails are matched to the order, the live tracking status pulled from one platform, ShipStation, EasyPost or the ShipHero tracking field, and a reply drafted in your template. Querying each carrier directly instead is a standard build.

    Where a person decides: A person approves replies to clients. End-customer replies may send automatically under rules you set.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  4. Rate card kept in one queryable table

    Carrier rate cards, fuel surcharges and your client price lists loaded into SQL Server from the PDFs and spreadsheets they arrive in, versioned by effective date, so quotes, client billing and the carrier invoice audit all read the same source.

    Where a person decides: Pricing changes are loaded by you; the table is read-only to every other system.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: LeadForge AI. Drafting behind a QA gate capped at two retries, with one typed state, is the LeadForge pattern. Built for B2B outreach, not freight quotes.

    Where a person decides: Unmatched and duplicate lines wait for a person with the reason. Nothing is approved for payment automatically.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Surcharges checked against the contract

    Every billed surcharge, residential, address correction, dimensional weight, fuel, checked against the rate card table under RFQs and the package data, with overcharges listed by invoice and carrier.

    Where a person decides: You decide what to dispute; the system builds the list.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Dispute pack drafted for the carrier

    Disputed lines from the surcharge check grouped by carrier and invoice into the carrier’s claim format, with tracking numbers, weights and the contract clause attached, ready to submit.

    Where a person decides: A person submits every dispute; the system assembles it.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  4. Freight spend and recharges in Power BI

    Carrier invoices, shipments and client recharges in one freight fact table by lane, service and client, reconciled to carrier statements, Power BI on top, with the recharge gap per shipment: client billed against carrier charged. One more table on the WMS sync; multi-week without it.

    Where a person decides: Finance signs off the reconciliation before the old spreadsheet is retired.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks

Logistics, fulfilment and 3PL · Inventory and stock accuracy

Still explaining to clients why the WMS and their Shopify show different stock?

  1. Stock parity between WMS and storefronts, daily

    On-hand and available quantities from ShipHero compared with Shopify, Amazon and WooCommerce per SKU every day through their APIs, with differences listed by cause: pending receipts, holds, unsynced adjustments.

    Where a person decides: A person decides each correction. The system lists and explains; it does not adjust stock.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Count variances traced to a cause

    When a cycle count in ShipHero shows a variance, the bin’s transactions since the last good count, receipts, picks, moves, adjustments, are pulled from the WMS log into SQL Server and the likely cause listed with the evidence: mis-pick, unreceived PO, adjustment never synced.

    Where a person decides: The inventory lead decides the adjustment; the system never writes stock on its own.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Days of cover and aged stock per client

    Sales velocity from orders and on-hand from the WMS give days of cover and aged stock per SKU per client, refreshed nightly in the fact tables, with a weekly list sent to each account manager.

    Where a person decides: Reorder and clear-out decisions stay with the client; the list informs.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. Monthly stock statement per client, reconciled

    Opening stock plus receipts and returns, minus shipments and adjustments, equals closing stock, per SKU per client, built from the fact tables monthly and compared with the client’s own count when they send one as an export. Differences listed by transaction.

    Where a person decides: The account manager sends the statement; disputed lines go to a person with the transactions attached.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: E-commerce Fulfillment ETL. Multi-cause data inflation and voided shipments traced to root cause in that pipeline, 68,890 orders reconciled. A retailer’s orders, not a 3PL’s stock.

Still assembling carrier damage claims by hand, weeks after the photo was taken?

  1. Returns logged in the WMS, not retyped

    Loop and Returnly already raise the RMA; the gap is your side. Their webhooks and client return emails are matched to the WMS order, the reason classified, and the return logged in ShipHero with photos attached, so the bench knows what is coming.

    Where a person decides: Refund and restock decisions follow the client’s rules; anything outside them waits for a person.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Return condition graded from inspection photos

    Photos taken at the returns bench are checked by a vision model against written grading rules, sellable, damaged, wrong item, and the proposed grade written with the evidence and the rule it matched.

    Where a person decides: A person confirms the grade before stock is restocked or written off.

    AI systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Carrier claims assembled, then chased to settlement

    For a lost or damaged shipment, the tracking history, declared value, packing photos and invoice are pulled into the carrier’s claim format and filed to the order. Open claims get a scheduled follow-up with the reference until a settlement or rejection arrives and is logged.

    Where a person decides: The claims desk submits each claim; rejections go to a person and accepting a settlement is a human decision.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  4. Accuracy chargebacks answered from the scan log

    When a client charges back a mis-pick or damaged delivery, the pick and pack scan log from ShipHero, the pack-bench photo if you take one, the weight check and the carrier scans are pulled together and a reply drafted with the evidence attached.

    Where a person decides: The account manager decides whether to accept the chargeback; the system only assembles the evidence.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks

Closest system I’ve shipped: Creative Generation Pipeline. A vision model checking every image against written rules, 100+ a day, capped retries then a person. Ad creatives, not returns.

Still spending weeks wiring each new client’s ERP, SKUs and retailer files into the WMS?

  1. Client ERP orders into the WMS

    ShipHero connects Shopify, Amazon and WooCommerce itself. This is for the rest: orders from a client’s NetSuite, SAP Business One, custom API or SFTP drop into the WMS through n8n with retries, idempotent writes and an error queue, shipment confirmations and tracking written back.

    Where a person decides: Orders that fail validation wait for a person; nothing is created twice.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. SKU mapping confirmed once, applied every time

    The client’s product list is matched to your SKU master with an 8-tier resolver built for messy names and codes; proposed matches are listed with confidence for review. The saved mapping then serves order sheets and ERP feeds alike.

    Where a person decides: A person confirms low-confidence matches once; the mapping is then remembered.

    AI systemsQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  3. HS codes proposed for new client SKUs

    ShipStation and your broker already produce commercial invoices and file declarations. The slow part is classifying new SKUs. Each product’s description, material and use from the client’s sheet is read and an HS code proposed with the reasoning and the tariff line, listed for review.

    Where a person decides: A person or your broker confirms every code before it reaches the product master.

    AI systemsQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  4. Retailer EDI files mapped to the WMS

    SPS Commerce or TrueCommerce handles AS2, acknowledgements and trading-partner testing. This maps the 850, 856 and 810 files they deliver by SFTP or API to and from ShipHero: orders in, ASNs and invoices out, every document logged and failures queued.

    Where a person decides: Your ops team sees every failed document in a queue with the reason; nothing is dropped silently.

    AutomationStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  5. Client portal with invoices and an assistant

    ShipHero shows clients their orders. This portal exists for what it does not: billing lines and invoices, SLA numbers and stock per client from the fact tables, and an assistant that answers only from that client’s data. Next.js and FastAPI; can run self-hosted and EU-resident.

    Where a person decides: Clients see only their data. Anything the assistant cannot answer from the data goes to the account manager.

    AI productsComplex system · multi-week

Closest system I’ve shipped: LeadForge AI. Next.js on FastAPI, agent output streamed to reviewers, is the LeadForge stack the portal reuses. Built for sales outreach, not a client portal.

Still setting next week’s picking roster from a gut feel about which clients will be busy?

  1. Units per hour and cost per order

    Pick, pack and ship timestamps per user from ShipHero joined to hours from the export of Deputy or When I Work, in the fact tables: units per hour by picker and shift, labour cost per order by client, refreshed nightly, in Power BI.

    Where a person decides: You define a billable hour and a unit; the report changes nothing in payroll or the WMS.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  2. Next week’s orders expected per client per day

    Order history by client and weekday from the fact tables, adjusted by the promotion dates clients send you, gives expected orders and units per client per day for the coming week. Plain statistics, not a model; the error against last week is shown beside it.

    Where a person decides: The ops lead sets the roster in Deputy or When I Work; the forecast informs, it schedules no one.

    Data systemsStandard build · 2–6 weeks
  3. Picker-hours needed per day, sent weekly

    The forecast above divided by your measured units per hour gives picker-hours and packer-hours needed per day and shift. Posted to Slack or Teams and the ops lead’s email each Thursday with the assumptions written out.

    Where a person decides: Shifts and names are decided by the ops lead; the message carries numbers, not a roster.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
  4. Carrier cut-off watch posted during the day

    At set times, orders in ShipHero not yet shipped are counted against each carrier’s pickup time and the client’s same-day promise, and a short message posts to Slack or Teams naming the clients and carriers at risk, with the order list linked.

    Where a person decides: The floor lead decides who moves to packing; the watch moves no orders and changes no priorities.

    AutomationQuick win · days to 2 weeks
